Research & Teaching

My research explores the design of learning experiences in higher education through the lens of 4E cognition, the view that cognition is embodied, embedded, enactive, and extended. I’m most interested in how it can inform instructional design and produce authentic, relevant learning experiences that prepare pre-professional students for their careers.

Selected studies

Video game tutorials and software training

In a grounded theory study, I looked at how video game tutorials are designed from a pedagogical point of view and what serious software training could learn from them. I recorded gameplay from seven popular games across different genres and broke each tutorial into discrete instructional units. Coding those 269 units produced 13 axial codes and a single selective theme, “designed situatedness,” which led to three design principles that could be applied to software training. I presented the results at the AECT International Conference.

VR rehearsal experience for pre-service music teachers

I filmed a music rehearsal with a stereoscopic camera, capturing a 180-degree view from the ensemble director’s position, and edited the footage into an immersive VR experience. Participants see the rehearsal from an expert director’s perspective, with visual cues that point out potential affordances in the room, so pre-service teachers can practice recognizing what an experienced director notices and how to respond. I piloted it with a pre-service teacher, recording their view in the headset, their spoken reactions, and a follow-up interview.

Teaching

My teaching starts from a question students ask all the time: “Why do we have to learn this?” I want students to be able to answer it for themselves, so I tie content to real problems outside the classroom, share the learning outcomes and the reasons behind them from the first day, and adjust the course as student feedback comes in. I also design for the students actually in the room, with outcomes broad enough for different backgrounds and goals, flexible deadlines, and room for revision.
