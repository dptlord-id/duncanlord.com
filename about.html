About

I’m an instructional designer at UGA Online. I partner with faculty to shape course content, media, and assessments. Then I build the custom HTML pages and web apps that present it in eLC. I also review course media and fix accessibility issues.

How I got here

I started my career as a high school band director at Morgan County Charter High School. Alongside teaching, I wrote curriculum, including KASH, a year-long soft-skills program taught by every teacher in the building. That project was my first real instructional design work. From there I went on to earn an M.Ed. in Learning, Design, and Technology at UGA.

At UGA I coordinated the Presentation Collaboratory Fellowship, a public speaking program for first-generation and rural undergraduates, and later supported learning technologies at the Center for Teaching and Learning, where I led workshops helping faculty use eLC and other campus learning technologies. I joined the Office of Online Learning after that.

Along the way I’ve also worked with private clients: revising more than 24 online courses across STEM and humanities disciplines, consulting on process improvement and employee onboarding for healthcare organizations, building an online content strategy for a real estate agent, and leading curriculum development and state accreditation for a UI/UX education firm.

How I work

I’m a supporter of learning experience design. Instruction should be effective and efficient, but it should also be engaging and easy to use, so the experience itself feels meaningful and relevant to the people going through it. Like a design thinking project, my work starts with understanding what learners actually experience before deciding what to build.

I also try to make the point of the learning obvious. When learners can answer “why do we have to learn this?” for themselves, they engage more and learn more deeply, so I tie content to the real situations people will use it in.

My research looks at how 4E cognition can inform instructional design, especially for students preparing to enter a profession. You can read more on my Research & Teaching page.
